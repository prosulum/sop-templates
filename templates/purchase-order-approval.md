# Purchase Order Approval

Updated September 28, 2026

This procedure takes one purchase from a written request to an approved, numbered purchase order that the vendor holds before they ship or start work. It exists so spend is checked against budget and approved by the right person before money is committed, not after the invoice arrives. The Requester submits the request, the Bookkeeper checks it, routes it, issues the purchase order and files it, and the Approver named for the amount approves or denies it. Start it every time someone wants to buy something at or above your no-PO threshold.

**Primary owner:** Bookkeeper  
**Runs:** Every purchase at or above your documented no-PO threshold  
**Time:** 10 to 15 minutes to submit a request; approval usually takes [N] business days per tier

## Before you start

- [Your purchasing tool, or a structured PO template and tracker] with unique PO numbering
- A written approval matrix: no PO needed below [amount 1, e.g. $500]; Tier 1 from [amount 1] up to [amount 2, e.g. $5,000], the Manager approves; Tier 2 at or above [amount 2], the Owner approves. "Approver" below means whoever the matrix names for the request amount.
- A list of purchase types that always go to the Owner whatever the amount: [always-Owner purchase types, e.g. software subscriptions, legal services, or contracts longer than a set number of months]
- Current department or project budgets to check requests against, and the Budget Holder named for each
- An approved vendor list with a W-9 and verified banking details on file for each vendor
- Roles used below (rename to match your team): Requester (anyone who asks to buy), Bookkeeper (checks, routes, issues, and files), Budget Holder (signs off on over-budget requests), Approver (Manager or Owner per the matrix)
- A backup Approver for each tier, named on your worksheet
- Approval wait limits set in advance: the Approver responds within [N] business days, and the backup Approver is asked after that

## Procedure

1. **Submit a purchase request with business justification** (Owner: Requester)

   - **a.** In [your purchasing tool], state what is being purchased, the estimated amount, the vendor, the business reason, and the department or project budget it should be charged to.
   - **b.** Attach any supporting quote or vendor proposal.
   - **c.** Submit the request. If the Bookkeeper returns it for missing information, correct it and submit it again from this step.

   *Why this matters:* Requiring justification up front puts each purchase in front of a decision-maker before money is committed.

2. **Check the request against the budget and the approved vendor list** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** Check that the request says what is being purchased, the amount, the vendor, and the business reason. If any is missing, return the request to the Requester with the message below and stop; the Requester restarts at step 1.
   - **b.** Compare the requested amount to the remaining budget for the department or project the Requester named in step 1.
   - **c.** If the request is over budget, send it to that budget's Budget Holder for sign-off, and continue only when the Budget Holder signs off in [your purchasing tool]. If the Budget Holder declines, tell the Requester the reason and stop.
   - **d.** Check the vendor against the approved vendor list. If the vendor is not on it, follow the new vendor entry in the troubleshooting section below, then return to this step.

   > **Use this wording: Request returned to the Requester**
   > 
   > Hello [Requester name],
   > 
   > I have returned your purchase request [request number] because it is missing [what is being purchased / the estimated amount / the vendor / the business reason]. Please add it and submit the request again. Nothing can be ordered until an approved PO is issued.
   > 
   > Thank you,
   > [Bookkeeper name]

   *Why this matters:* Checking budget before approval catches overruns while there is still a decision to make, not at month end.

3. **Choose the Approver for the amount and confirm it is not the Requester** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** Find the request amount in the approval matrix and note the Approver for that tier. If the purchase type is on the always-Owner list, the Approver is the Owner.
   - **b.** Check that the Approver is not the person who made the request, and ideally not the person who will receive or inspect the goods.
   - **c.** If the Approver is the Requester, route the request one tier up. If that is also the Requester, follow the small-team entry in the troubleshooting section below.
   - **d.** Send the request to the Approver through the approval workflow in [your purchasing tool].
   - **e.** If the Approver has not responded within [N] business days, send the request to the backup Approver for that tier named on your worksheet.

   *Why this matters:* Checking this before approval prevents unauthorized or fictitious purchases; without it, approval is a formality rather than a real check.

4. **Approve or deny the request** (Owner: Approver)

   - **a.** Read the request, the quote, and the budget and vendor checks recorded in step 2.
   - **b.** To approve, click approve in [your purchasing tool]. The timestamped record in the tool is the approval; a verbal yes or an email does not count.
   - **c.** To deny, enter the reason in the tool and send the denial message to the Requester. The request ends here, and the tool records it as denied. If the Requester still needs the purchase, they submit a new request at step 1.

   > **Use this wording: Denial to the Requester**
   > 
   > Hello [Requester name],
   > 
   > Your purchase request [request number] for [description] at [amount] was not approved. Reason: [reason]. If you still need this, you can submit a new request with [what would change the decision]. Please do not order from the vendor.
   > 
   > Thank you,
   > [Approver name]

   *Why this matters:* A recorded decision, whether yes or no, makes approval a control instead of a habit.

5. **Issue the PO with a unique number** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** In [your purchasing tool], generate the purchase order with the next sequential number, the vendor details, a line-item description, quantity, unit price, and total.
   - **b.** Check that the PO total equals the approved amount. If it does not, do not send it; return to step 4 so the Approver can approve the new amount.
   - **c.** Send the PO to the vendor before they ship goods or begin work.
   - **d.** Save a copy in [where purchase orders are filed] right after issuing it.

   > **Use this wording: PO sent to the vendor**
   > 
   > Hello [vendor contact],
   > 
   > Attached is purchase order [PO number] for [description], total [amount]. Please confirm you can supply this by [delivery date]. Put the PO number on your invoice and on the delivery paperwork. Please do not ship or start work until you have this PO.
   > 
   > Thank you,
   > [Bookkeeper name], [your company name]

   *Why this matters:* An unnumbered or informally issued PO is a suggestion, not a control; vendors and your own AP team need a documented reference.

6. **File the PO and log receipt** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** Store the approved PO in [where purchase orders are filed] so it can be found by number.
   - **b.** Note the expected delivery or receipt date on the PO record.
   - **c.** When the goods or services arrive, log the receipt on the PO record with the date and who received them. If they do not arrive by the expected date, contact the vendor and note the answer on the record.
   - **d.** At invoice time, the Vendor Payment procedure uses this PO and the receipt record for its three-way match.

   *Why this matters:* A PO nobody can find at invoice time is a control that exists only on paper.

7. **Close out the PO after payment** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** When the invoice has been matched and paid under the Vendor Payment procedure, mark the PO closed.
   - **b.** Note any difference between the PO amount and the final invoice, and record the reason.
   - **c.** If the difference is over [the variance limit, e.g. 5 percent], send the difference back to the Approver for a new approval before it is paid.

   *Why this matters:* Closing each PO against its invoice keeps the budget control reliable over time.

## Exceptions and troubleshooting

- **If** A purchase is needed faster than the normal approval turnaround (rush purchase). **Then:** First, the Requester writes the reason and the deadline in the request and marks it rush in [your purchasing tool]. Second, the Bookkeeper sends it straight to the Approver for the amount, or to the backup Approver if that person is not available today. Third, the Approver approves or denies it the same day in [the channel your team uses for urgent approvals], and the Bookkeeper or Requester records the decision in the tool within [N] business days. Fourth, the Bookkeeper issues the PO as in step 5. A verbal yes with no written record does not count, and nothing is ordered before the approval is written down.
- **If** The final invoice comes in higher than the approved PO amount. **Then:** Hold payment, ask the Requester and the vendor to explain the difference, and record the explanation on the PO record. If the difference is over [the variance limit, e.g. 5 percent], send it back to the Approver for a new approval before it is paid, as in step 7.
- **If** A request is for a vendor not on the approved vendor list (new vendor). **Then:** First, hold the request. Second, ask the vendor for a W-9 or equivalent and bank details. Third, phone the vendor at a number you found independently, not one on their invoice or email, and confirm the bank details out loud. Fourth, the Approver for the amount signs off on adding the vendor in [where the sign-off is recorded]. Fifth, add the vendor to the approved vendor list. Then return to step 2. If the vendor has not sent the W-9 and bank details within [N] business days, tell the Requester and leave the request on hold.
- **If** The Requester is the only person who can approve (small team). **Then:** Route the request to a second person named on your worksheet, who records the approval. The Owner reviews a sample of [N] POs each [period] after the fact and records any problems found.
- **If** Monthly review of closed POs against budget. **Then:** Run this as its own short procedure on a fixed day each month ([your monthly PO review procedure]): list the POs closed in the month, compare each to its budget, and report any budget drift to the Budget Holder.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every request includes what is being purchased, the estimated amount, the vendor, and a business reason
- [ ] Request checked against the current department or project budget, with Budget Holder sign-off if over budget
- [ ] Vendor found on the approved vendor list, or added through the new vendor entry
- [ ] Approver chosen from the approval matrix and the always-Owner list, and confirmed not to be the Requester
- [ ] Approval or denial recorded in the tool with a timestamp
- [ ] PO issued with a unique sequential number, complete line-item detail, and a total equal to the approved amount
- [ ] PO sent to the vendor before work begins or goods ship
- [ ] PO filed, receipt logged, and matched against the invoice at payment time, with variances recorded

## Common mistakes

- **Mistake:** Work starts or goods ship before the PO is approved and issued. **Fix:** Tell vendors and Requesters that nothing is ordered until a PO is issued. Anything ordered without one gets flagged for after-the-fact review, not treated as normal.
- **Mistake:** The Requester approves their own purchase. **Fix:** Build the approval matrix into the tool so self-approval is not possible, and use step 3 to route to another person every time.
- **Mistake:** Verbal or email "go ahead" approvals with no record in the purchasing tool. **Fix:** Require every approval to be captured in the tool so there is a timestamped record, not an email thread nobody can find later.
- **Mistake:** Approved POs go unmatched at invoice time because nobody can find them. **Fix:** File every approved PO in [where purchase orders are filed] with the same numbering convention so AP can pull it in seconds during the three-way match.
- **Mistake:** Issuing the PO for a different amount than the one the Approver approved. **Fix:** Compare the PO total to the approved amount in step 5 before sending, and return to step 4 if it differs.

## How to know it is working

- No vendor payment is made without a matching approved PO for purchases at or above the no-PO threshold.
- Every approval is recorded in the tool, not verbal.
- Department spend stays within budget because requests are checked before commitment, not after.
- Closed POs reconcile cleanly against invoices, with recorded reasons for any variance.
