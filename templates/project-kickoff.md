# Project Kickoff

Updated September 28, 2026

This SOP takes one project from a signed or approved scope to a signed charter, a kickoff meeting, a published recap, an open shared workspace, and a hand-off to Project Management. The Project manager runs it once per project, triggered by an approved scope or signed statement of work, and it applies to any internal or client project involving more than a couple of people. It works because the charter is signed before the meeting is scheduled, so everyone starts from the same written scope.

**What this gives you:** A project that starts with everyone agreeing in writing on what it is for gives the people you asked to build it room to do their best work, and the thing you imagined starts taking shape in their hands.

**Primary owner:** Project manager  
**Runs:** Once per project, triggered by an approved scope or signed statement of work  
**Time:** [Your prep time, e.g. 3 to 5 hours], plus a [your meeting length, e.g. 45 to 60 minute] kickoff meeting

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the scope is signed and draft the one-page project charter** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Open the scope document at [scope document location] and confirm it is signed or approved by [approver role]. If it is not, stop and tell [approver role]; do not draft the charter until it is.
   - **b.** Write the project goal, what is in scope and out of scope, and what "done" looks like in measurable terms, using [charter template title and location]. Add a "Signed by" line with a date and a "Confirmed by" line for attendees.
   - **c.** List the target completion date. Leave the kickoff date and milestone dates for steps 4 and 7.
   - **d.** Keep it to one page. If it needs more than one page, shorten the wording and keep the detail in the scope document. If the scope itself is too large to summarize on one page, ask [approver role] whether to split the project.

   *Why this matters:* A charter that lives on paper, not in memory, keeps scope from expanding once work starts.

2. **Get the charter signed before you schedule the kickoff** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Send the charter to [approver role] using the message below.
   - **b.** The signature and date on the charter are the record. Do not schedule the kickoff meeting until it is signed.
   - **c.** If [approver role] asks for changes, make them, send the charter again, and repeat this step.
   - **d.** If [approver role] declines to sign, stop the kickoff and tell [sponsor role]. Do not continue until you have a signed charter or a written instruction from [sponsor role].
   - **e.** If there is no reply within [reminder wait, in business days] business days, send a reminder. If there is still no reply after another [reminder wait, in business days] business days, tell [sponsor role] and pause the kickoff.
   - **f.** When it is signed, save the signed charter in [project folder location].

   > **Use this wording: Charter sign-off request**
   > 
   > Attached is the one-page charter for [project name], built from the signed scope. Please read it and reply "signed" or send changes by [date]. The kickoff meeting will not be scheduled until it is signed.

   *Why this matters:* A charter nobody approved is only the project manager's opinion; the signature makes it the agreement the team works inside.

3. **Build the RACI for every major deliverable** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** List each major deliverable or workstream down one side, using [RACI template title and location].
   - **b.** Assign a single Responsible person and exactly one Accountable owner for each. Shared accountability means no accountability.
   - **c.** Note who needs to be Consulted before a decision and who just needs to be Informed after.
   - **d.** If a deliverable ends up with no Accountable owner or with two, go back to [sponsor role] for a decision and update the RACI before continuing.

   *Why this matters:* Ambiguity about who can make a call stalls a project even when everyone is working hard.

4. **Set the milestone timeline with dates, not durations** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Build the timeline around calendar dates for each milestone, not "two weeks after kickoff" style estimates.
   - **b.** Assign an owner to every milestone, not just the project as a whole.
   - **c.** Add a buffer of [percent, e.g. 10 to 15 percent] to the overall deadline to absorb the first unexpected delay.
   - **d.** If the timeline already looks tight, increase the buffer or resequence the milestones now, and note the change on the timeline. If the buffer moves the target completion date on the signed charter, send the charter again under step 2.

   *Why this matters:* Duration estimates drift the moment anything starts late. Dates with named owners make a slip visible sooner.

5. **Open the risk log and capture known risks** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Brainstorm risks with [core team roles]: what could delay, derail, or blow the budget on this project. Record them in [risk log template title and location].
   - **b.** Assign an owner and a mitigation step to each risk. A risk with no owner just sits there until it happens.
   - **c.** Set a cadence to revisit the log, ideally at the regular check-in that step 6 sets for the rest of the project.

   *Why this matters:* Writing risks down while they are hypothetical is cheaper than discovering them live.

6. **Set the communication plan** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Define the check-in cadence, the channel used for updates, and who receives what.
   - **b.** Name the escalation path: write the role to contact first when something is blocked or off track, and the role after that.
   - **c.** Save the plan in [project folder location] next to the charter.

   *Why this matters:* Without a stated plan, communication can default to whoever is loudest or most anxious, not the people who need the information.

7. **Send the kickoff pre-read [2 to 3] business days ahead** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Package the signed charter, scope, timeline, RACI, risk log, and meeting agenda into one packet.
   - **b.** Send it with the message below, far enough ahead that attendees can review it, not just skim it in the meeting.
   - **c.** Ask each required attendee to reply "confirmed". If one has not replied [confirmation deadline, in business days] business days before the meeting, call that person. If one cannot attend, reschedule the meeting and send the packet again with the new date.

   > **Use this wording: Pre-read message**
   > 
   > The kickoff for [project name] is on [date] at [time]. The packet is attached: charter, scope, timeline, RACI, risk log, and agenda. Please read it before the meeting and reply "confirmed" by [date].

   *Why this matters:* A kickoff meeting spent reading documents for the first time is a kickoff meeting that produces no real alignment.

8. **Run the kickoff meeting with all stakeholders** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Walk through scope, roles, timeline, and the communication plan with everyone who executes or approves work.
   - **b.** Ask each attendee in turn whether scope, their role and the timeline are clear, and write the name of every attendee who says yes in the "Confirmed by" line of the charter.
   - **c.** If an attendee disagrees on scope or roles, write the objection down and resolve it with [approver role] before the meeting ends. If it is not resolved, mark the charter "open" and return to step 2 for a new signature.
   - **d.** When the revised charter is signed, redo any of steps 3 to 6 that the change touched, then go straight to step 7 to send the changed charter and reschedule the meeting. Do not go to step 9.
   - **e.** Capture decisions and any changes to the charter or RACI live, in the document itself.

   *Why this matters:* The kickoff is the cheapest place in the whole project to catch a misunderstanding.

9. **Publish the recap, open the shared workspace, and hand off to Project Management** (Owner: Project manager)

   - **a.** Send a written recap within [recap deadline, in hours] hours of the meeting: confirmed scope, roles, milestones, and next steps. Send it to everyone on [stakeholder list location], attendees and non-attendees.
   - **b.** Confirm the project folder at [project folder location] holds every document from this SOP. Set up the task tracker and the communication channel before day one of real execution.
   - **c.** Load the first milestone into the tracker with its owner and due date already assigned.
   - **d.** Hand the running project to Project Management: give the person named to run Project Management (if that is you, say so in the recap) the signed charter, RACI, milestone timeline, risk log, and communication plan, and tell them where each one is stored. Project Management picks up the status cadence, change requests, the risk and issue log, and closeout.

   *Why this matters:* A live workspace on day one means the team starts working in the system, not in someone's inbox, and the hand-off tells the person running the project exactly what they inherit.

## Exceptions and troubleshooting

- **If** Stakeholders raise goals during kickoff that fall outside the signed scope. **Then:** Note them in the meeting, confirm the current scope stays as signed, and log the new ask as a change request for Project Management to evaluate separately. Do not silently absorb it.
- **If** Two people both believe they own the same decision. **Then:** Resolve it against the RACI in the room. There is exactly one Accountable owner; if the RACI is unclear, fix it on the spot before the meeting ends.
- **If** The milestone timeline already looks tight before work has even started. **Then:** Increase the buffer or resequence the milestones now. A timeline that is already at risk on day one is likely to get worse under real conditions.
- **If** The approver signs the charter but a stakeholder later says they never agreed to the scope. **Then:** Do not change the signed charter. Take the objection to [approver role], and if scope changes, record it as a change request under Project Management and get a new signature on the updated charter.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Scope document confirmed signed or approved before the charter was drafted
- [ ] Charter signed by the approver, with signature and date, before the kickoff meeting was scheduled
- [ ] RACI complete for every major deliverable, with exactly one Accountable owner each
- [ ] Milestone timeline has calendar dates and a named owner per milestone, not estimates
- [ ] Risk log includes the top known risks with an owner and mitigation step for each
- [ ] Communication plan states cadence, channel, and escalation path
- [ ] Kickoff pre-read sent [2 to 3] business days in advance and required attendees confirmed
- [ ] Every attendee named in the "Confirmed by" line of the charter, or the charter marked "open"
- [ ] Kickoff recap sent within the set number of hours of the meeting to everyone on the stakeholder list
- [ ] Project workspace (folder, tracker, channel) live and the project handed to Project Management before day one of execution

## Common mistakes

- **Mistake:** Running the kickoff meeting without a written charter, so scope lives in people's memory and can drift within weeks. **Fix:** Write the one-page charter first, get it signed, and put it in front of everyone at the meeting. No signed charter, no kickoff.
- **Mistake:** Scheduling the kickoff while the charter is still waiting for a signature. **Fix:** Step 2 blocks the schedule until the charter is signed; if the signature is late, follow the reminder and escalation path in step 2.
- **Mistake:** Skipping the RACI because the team is small, then hitting a decision nobody knows they are allowed to make. **Fix:** Build the RACI even for a three-person project. It takes little time and can prevent a much longer stall later.
- **Mistake:** Building the timeline on duration guesses instead of committed calendar dates. **Fix:** Convert every estimate to an actual date with an owner before the kickoff meeting, not after.
- **Mistake:** Leaving the risk log empty because nothing has gone wrong yet. **Fix:** Brainstorm risks as a standing kickoff agenda item. An empty risk log more likely means no one looked than that there are no risks.

## How to know it is working

- Every team member can state the project's definition of done in one sentence.
- Every workstream has exactly one Accountable owner, confirmed in the RACI.
- The signed charter, with signature and date, is stored in the project folder.
- The team is already working inside the shared workspace by the end of week one.
- The top known risks are logged with an owner and mitigation before real work begins.

## Make it your procedure

1. Have the person who does this work fill in the [bracketed] parts and correct the steps. They know the details an owner skips.
2. Hand it to someone who has never done the task and watch them follow it without help. Every question they ask is a missing step. Add it.
3. When the work changes, change the procedure first, update the date under the title, and tell the people who use it what changed. Then nobody has a reason to work around it.
4. Save it with the others in one shared procedures folder, and fill in the procedure record at the bottom.
