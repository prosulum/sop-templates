# Client Invoicing

Updated September 28, 2026

This procedure takes one client invoice from a confirmed billing trigger to a payment matched in the books. It exists so each invoice goes out accurate and on time, and so a late payer gets the same follow-up every time. The Bookkeeper creates the invoice, a Reviewer checks and initials it, and the Bookkeeper then sends it, posts it, follows up if it is late, and matches the payment when it arrives. Run it each time a billing trigger occurs: a deliverable or milestone is complete, or a billing period closes.

**What this gives you:** The money conversation stops standing between you and your clients. Every invoice goes out the same way, so your attention stays on the craft that earned it and the business becomes known for work clients are glad to pay for.

**Primary owner:** Bookkeeper  
**Runs:** Every billing cycle: weekly, biweekly, monthly, or per milestone, depending on the contract  
**Time:** [e.g. 15 to 30] minutes per invoice, plus follow-up time on any invoice that is past due; replace with your own after three timed runs

## Before you start

- [Your accounting software] with the client billing profile set up
- The signed contract or engagement letter with rate, scope, and payment terms on file
- A standing invoice numbering convention and invoice template
- The client billing profile: legal name, billing address, [client billing contact], required delivery channel (email, portal, or AP inbox), and any PO number the client requires
- Agreed payment terms for each client (Net 15, Net 30, due on receipt, and so on)
- A chart of accounts that lists the income account for each service you bill
- Roles used below (rename to match your team): Bookkeeper creates and sends, Reviewer is a second person who did not create the invoice, Account Manager owns the client relationship, Owner approves payment plans and posting questions; name a backup approver: [backup approver]
- A follow-up schedule set in advance: first reminder [N1] days past due (e.g. 7), second reminder [N2] days past due (e.g. 14), phone call [N3] days past due (e.g. 21)
- How long the Owner has to answer a question before you go to the backup approver: [N4] business days
- Three more numbers set in advance: the new due date for a short payment, [N5] days out; the send deadline, [N6] business days after the confirmed trigger; and the Owner's sample size, [N7] invoices each [period]
- Two places named in advance: [where the Reviewer initials an invoice, e.g. an approval field on the invoice record] and [your invoice log, e.g. the invoice record in your accounting software]

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the invoice trigger and gather billing details** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** Check [your project record or time log] to confirm the deliverable or milestone is complete, or that the billing period has closed.
   - **b.** Open the signed contract or SOW and note the rate, what is covered, the payment terms, and any reimbursable expenses to pass through.
   - **c.** Check the client billing profile for anything that changed since the last invoice: a scope change, an agreed discount, or a credit owed.
   - **d.** If the trigger is not confirmed, do not create the invoice. Tell the Account Manager exactly what is missing, then stop. Restart at step 1 when the Account Manager confirms in writing.

   *Why this matters:* If the trigger is not confirmed, the client can dispute the invoice and you have to reissue it.

2. **Create and number the invoice** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** In [your accounting software], open the client billing profile and start a new invoice. Save it as a draft; do not send it.
   - **b.** Give it the next unused number under the standing convention (e.g. INV-2026-014). Do not reuse or skip a number.
   - **c.** Add one line per service or item with a description, quantity, and amount that match the contract, so the client can match each line to the work performed.
   - **d.** Set the payment terms and due date from the contract, and add the PO number or reference code if the client requires one.

   *Why this matters:* A numbered, itemized invoice lets the client's AP team match each line to the work without asking for clarification.

3. **Have the invoice reviewed and initialed before it goes out** (Owner: Reviewer)

   - **a.** Check the invoice total against the contract or the approved scope for the period.
   - **b.** Check the client legal name, billing address, billing contact, and required reference numbers against the billing profile.
   - **c.** If everything matches, enter your initials and the date in [where the Reviewer initials an invoice].
   - **d.** If you find an error, write it in the invoice notes and return the invoice to the Bookkeeper. The Bookkeeper corrects it, and the Reviewer repeats this step from the first check. Do not send an invoice that has no Reviewer initials.

   *Why this matters:* A second person checks the amount and every line before the client sees the invoice.

4. **Send the invoice through the client's required channel and log it** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** Send the invoice through the channel in the billing profile: email to [client billing contact], the client's portal, or the client's AP inbox.
   - **b.** Copy the Account Manager so they know billing went out and can answer client questions.
   - **c.** Save proof of sending to the invoice record: the sent email or the portal's submission confirmation number.
   - **d.** Enter the send date, amount, and due date in [your invoice log].
   - **e.** If the email bounces or the portal rejects the invoice, phone the billing contact listed on the signed contract, correct the billing profile, resend, and note the correction in [your invoice log].

   > **Use this wording: Invoice email**
   > 
   > Subject: Invoice [invoice number] from [your company name], due [due date]
   > 
   > Hello [client billing contact],
   > 
   > Attached is invoice [invoice number] for [description of work or billing period], totaling [amount]. Payment is due [due date] under [payment terms].
   > 
   > [Payment instructions.] If your team needs a PO number, reference code, or a different delivery address on the invoice, reply to this email and we will reissue it the same day.
   > 
   > Thank you,\
   > [Bookkeeper name], [your company name]

   *Why this matters:* If the invoice goes to the wrong contact or channel, the client may never see it and the due date passes unnoticed.

5. **Post the invoice in the books** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** Post the invoice to [the income account for this service in your chart of accounts].
   - **b.** If you use accrual accounting, confirm the invoice date falls in the period the work was performed. If it does not, stop and ask the Owner how to post it. If the Owner has not answered in [N4] business days, ask [backup approver]. Then continue.
   - **c.** Tag the invoice with the client and the project using [the client and project fields in your accounting software], so it appears in the right place in reports.

   *Why this matters:* Posting the invoice correctly on day one means month-end close starts from records that are already right.

6. **Check past-due invoices each week and follow the reminder schedule** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** Each [day of the week], run the accounts receivable aging report in [your accounting software] and list every invoice past due. For each one, check for a payment against it. If it is paid, go to step 7.
   - **b.** If it is not paid [N1] days past the due date, send the first reminder.
   - **c.** If it is not paid [N2] days past the due date, send the second reminder and copy the Account Manager.
   - **d.** If it is not paid [N3] days past the due date, phone the billing contact, then tell the Account Manager the outcome the same business day.
   - **e.** Record every contact attempt (date, method, person reached, outcome) in the client billing profile.
   - **f.** If the client disputes the invoice at any point, stop the reminders and follow the dispute entry in the troubleshooting section below. If the client asks for a payment plan, follow the payment plan entry there.

   > **Use this wording: First reminder ([N1] days past due)**
   > 
   > Subject: Reminder: invoice [invoice number], due [due date]
   > 
   > Hello [client billing contact],
   > 
   > A quick reminder that invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was due on [due date] and we have not yet received payment. A copy is attached.
   > 
   > If payment is already on its way, thank you, and please disregard this note. If something on the invoice needs correcting, reply and let us know.
   > 
   > [Bookkeeper name], [your company name]

   > **Use this wording: Second reminder ([N2] days past due)**
   > 
   > Subject: Second notice: invoice [invoice number], [number] days past due
   > 
   > Hello [client billing contact],
   > 
   > Invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was due on [due date] and is now [number] days past due. Please send payment by [date], or reply with the date we should expect it.
   > 
   > If there is a question about the invoice, [Account Manager name] is copied and can help.
   > 
   > [Bookkeeper name], [your company name]

   > **Use this wording: Phone call ([N3] days past due)**
   > 
   > Hello [client billing contact], this is [your name] from [your company name]. I am calling about invoice [invoice number] for [amount], which was due on [due date]. Do you know where it stands on your side? [Listen.] Can you give me a date we can expect payment? [Write down the date and the answer.] Thank you. I will note this and follow up on [date].

   *Why this matters:* The same documented follow-up each time gives every client the same clear payment expectation.

7. **Match the payment and close out the invoice** (Owner: Bookkeeper)

   - **a.** When a payment arrives, match it to the invoice number in [your accounting software] and record the deposit date and deposit reference on the invoice.
   - **b.** Check that the amount you applied equals the amount received.
   - **c.** If the invoice is paid in full, mark it paid and stop.
   - **d.** If the payment is short or a credit applies, apply what arrived, record the remaining balance as open with a new due date [N5] days out, and tell the Account Manager the same business day. Then go to step 6 for the remaining balance.
   - **e.** If a payment does not match any open invoice, record it as unapplied, tell the Account Manager the same business day, and do not apply it until the payer is confirmed.

   *Why this matters:* An unmatched payment leaves an invoice showing as open after it has been paid, so your receivables list does not show your real cash position.

## Exceptions and troubleshooting

- **If** A client disputes an invoice amount. **Then:** Stop the reminders. Pull the contract or SOW and any time or project records, walk through the difference line by line with the Account Manager, and tell the client the outcome in writing. If an error is confirmed, issue a credit memo instead of deleting and reissuing the invoice, then send the corrected balance and return to step 6. If no error is found, send the client the supporting records and resume step 6 from the point where you stopped.
- **If** A client says they never received the invoice. **Then:** Check the proof of sending saved in step 4 and the delivery channel on the billing profile. Resend using the invoice email wording, then phone the billing contact or check the portal to confirm it arrived this time. Restart the reminder schedule from the date of the resend.
- **If** A client requests a payment plan on an overdue balance. **Then:** Write the plan down with specific dates and amounts and send it to the Owner for sign-off. If the Owner has not replied within [N4] business days, ask [backup approver]. If neither replies within a further [N4] business days, tell the Account Manager and keep step 6 running. Once signed off, record the plan in the client billing profile so each installment gets its own due date in step 6.
- **If** A partial payment comes in. **Then:** Follow step 7: apply it, record the remaining balance with its own due date, and tell the Account Manager the same business day. Chase the difference under step 6 with the new due date.
- **If** You are not sure which invoices need step 6. **Then:** This is the first action in step 6. Each [day of the week], run the accounts receivable aging report in [your accounting software], list every invoice past due, and send each one through step 6 at its next reminder.
- **If** There is no second person available to be the Reviewer. **Then:** Name a backup Reviewer on your worksheet before you need one. If your team has only one person, the Owner reviews and initials a sample of [N7] sent invoices each [period] after they go out and records any errors found.

## Common mistakes

- **Mistake:** Invoicing before confirming the deliverable or billing period is complete. **Fix:** Do not create the invoice until the trigger is verified against the contract or project record.
- **Mistake:** Sending the invoice to whichever contact you happen to have instead of the client's AP channel. **Fix:** Confirm the required delivery channel, contact, and any PO or reference requirement once per client, and record it in the billing profile.
- **Mistake:** Letting overdue invoices sit without a documented follow-up schedule. **Fix:** Follow step 6 and the same [N1] / [N2] / [N3] day schedule every time, not only when cash gets tight.
- **Mistake:** One person creates, sends, and matches every invoice with no second set of eyes. **Fix:** Keep the Reviewer initials in step 3 on every invoice. If your team is too small for that, use the small-team entry in the troubleshooting section.
- **Mistake:** Sending the invoice as soon as it is created, before the Reviewer has initialed it. **Fix:** Save every new invoice as a draft in step 2 and send it only after step 3 is complete.

## How to know it is working

- Every invoice goes out within [N6] business days of the confirmed trigger, initialed by a Reviewer, with no missing line items.
- No invoice ages past [N3] days without a recorded contact attempt.
- Payments match invoices with zero unexplained variances at month end.
- Invoices match the agreed scope and contract.

## Make it your procedure

1. Have the person who does this work fill in the [bracketed] parts and correct the steps. They know the details an owner skips.
2. Hand it to someone who has never done the task and watch them follow it without help. Every question they ask is a missing step. Add it.
3. When the work changes, change the procedure first, update the date under the title, and tell the people who use it what changed. Then nobody has a reason to work around it.
4. Save it with the others in one shared procedures folder, and fill in the procedure record at the bottom.
