# Payroll Processing

Updated September 28, 2026

This procedure runs one payroll cycle from the cutoff reminder through submission and filing of the records, for both W-2 employees and 1099 contractors. It exists so people are paid the right amount on the right day, and so no one person both enters the pay data and approves the submission. The Payroll Admin collects and enters the data, a named Second Reviewer checks the payroll register and signs off, and the Payroll Admin submits and files. Run it once every pay period, starting on the reminder date on your payroll calendar (weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly).

**Primary owner:** Payroll Admin  
**Runs:** Every pay period, on your published payroll calendar  
**Time:** 1 to 2 hours per pay period; longer on the first run or when adding new hires

## Before you start

- Access to [your payroll platform] with the correct user role
- Current employee and contractor roster with pay rates, classification (W-2 or 1099), and banking details on file
- Approved timesheets or an hours source ([your time tracking system] or manager sign-off) for the pay period
- A documented exception list for the pay period: bonuses, commissions, deductions, garnishments, expense reimbursements
- The payroll calendar with the reminder date, cutoff date, and pay date for every period of the year
- Roles used below (rename to match your team): Payroll Admin, Second Reviewer (a named person other than the Payroll Admin), Manager (approves hours for their team), Owner (approves exceptions and decides on a missed cutoff)
- A backup Second Reviewer, named on your worksheet
- Three limits set in advance: the reminder lead time [N] business days before cutoff (e.g. 2 to 3), the overtime limit of [N] hours per person per period, above which a Manager must confirm, and the variance limit [variance limit, e.g. 5 percent] between this period's payroll total and the prior period's
- [Where the payroll records are filed, e.g. one folder on your shared drive] for the register, exception list, and sign-off

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the payroll calendar and send the cutoff reminder** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Open the payroll calendar and find this period's cutoff and pay date.
   - **b.** On the reminder date, [N] business days before the cutoff, send the cutoff reminder to Managers and hourly employees.
   - **c.** List any new hires, terminations, or pay changes that must be reflected this cycle, and note where each one is documented.

   > **Use this wording: Cutoff reminder to Managers and hourly employees**
   > 
   > Subject: Payroll cutoff [cutoff date] for pay date [pay date]
   > 
   > Hello [name or team],
   > 
   > Payroll for [pay period start] to [pay period end] closes on [cutoff date] at [time]. By then, we need approved hours for every person on your team. We also need any bonus, commission, or reimbursement to include this period, and the name of who approved it. Send them to [Payroll Admin name] at [where to send].
   > 
   > Hours that are estimated or not approved cannot be entered, and payment for them moves to the next pay period.
   > 
   > Thank you,
   > [Payroll Admin name]

   *Why this matters:* A missed cutoff either delays pay or forces an expensive off-cycle correction; a reminder before the cutoff is the simplest way to prevent both.

2. **Collect and verify approved hours** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Pull hours from [your time tracking system] or collect the Manager-approved timesheets.
   - **b.** Check each person's hours for missing punches, duplicate entries, and overtime above the overtime limit.
   - **c.** For each problem found, remove that person's line from this batch, send it back to the Manager with the message below, and record it in the open items list for this period as held. Continue with the remaining lines.
   - **d.** Do not accept estimated or unapproved hours. Return them to the Manager for sign-off first, in the same way.
   - **e.** When a Manager returns a corrected, approved line before the cutoff, add it back to the batch and go on to step 3.
   - **f.** If the corrected line has not come back by the cutoff, leave it out of this batch and tell the Second Reviewer it is held.
   - **g.** The Payroll Admin then pays it in the next period or in an off-cycle payment, as set by [your pay policy for late hours].

   > **Use this wording: Hours returned to a Manager**
   > 
   > Hello [Manager name],
   > 
   > I have held [employee name]'s hours for [pay period] out of this payroll because [reason: a missing punch on a date, a duplicate entry on a date, or overtime above the limit, with the hours]. Please send corrected, approved hours by [cutoff date and time]. If they arrive after that, they will be paid in [next pay period or off-cycle payment].
   > 
   > Thank you,
   > [Payroll Admin name]

   *Why this matters:* Unapproved or estimated hours are a common source of payroll errors, and they are harder to fix after money has moved.

3. **Verify contractor invoices, new hires, rate changes, and terminations** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Match every contractor invoice against the rate and scope on file. If one does not match, hold that invoice out of the batch and send it back to the contractor with the difference stated.
   - **b.** Confirm each new hire is fully set up in [your payroll platform] (classification, tax withholding forms, direct deposit) before their first pay run, following the Hiring Process procedure. If setup is not complete, hold that person out of this batch and tell the Second Reviewer.
   - **c.** Apply each approved rate change or raise effective this period, and confirm the effective date matches the change record.
   - **d.** Apply each termination effective this period, including final pay items, following the Employee Offboarding procedure.

   *Why this matters:* Classification and rate errors repeat every pay period until someone catches them, so catch them at the source.

4. **Enter hours and apply documented exceptions** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Enter the approved hours, salaries, and contractor payments into [your payroll platform].
   - **b.** Apply exceptions (bonuses, commissions, deductions, garnishments, reimbursements) only from the documented exception list for this period, not from memory or a side conversation. If someone asks for an exception that is not on the list, decline it for this period and ask them to get it approved and added.
   - **c.** Save a copy of the exception list to [where the payroll records are filed] with this period's payroll records.

   *Why this matters:* A documented exception list creates an audit trail and stops "just this once" changes from becoming undocumented habits.

5. **Run a second-person review and get sign-off before submission** (Owner: Second Reviewer)

   - **a.** Open the payroll register. Check headcount, hours per person, pay rates, deductions, and total cost.
   - **b.** Compare this period's total to the prior period's. If the difference is more than the variance limit, find the cause in the payroll data before you continue, and write the explanation next to the register.
   - **c.** If everything is explained, sign off in [where the Second Reviewer signs off, e.g. an approval in the payroll platform or a signed email], with your name and the date.
   - **d.** If you find an error, return the register to the Payroll Admin with the error written down. The Payroll Admin corrects it in step 4, and you repeat this step from the beginning.
   - **e.** If you have not signed off by [N] hours before the cutoff, the Payroll Admin sends the register to the backup Second Reviewer. If neither can review before the cutoff, do not submit without a review. Follow the missed cutoff entry in the troubleshooting section below.

   *Why this matters:* A second reviewer catches the errors the preparer is too close to see. It is the one check in this process that does not depend on the preparer being right.

6. **Submit payroll and confirm transmission** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Check that the payroll total on screen equals the total the Second Reviewer signed off. If it differs, do not submit; return to step 5.
   - **b.** Submit payroll in [your payroll platform] before the cutoff and record the confirmation number or submission timestamp.
   - **c.** Confirm that direct deposit transmissions were accepted and that contractor ACH or check payments were started. If any were rejected, fix the bank detail with the employee or contractor by phone or in person, then resubmit that payment and record the new confirmation.
   - **d.** Check that the expected settlement date matches the pay date on the calendar. If it does not, contact [your payroll provider] the same business day.

   *Why this matters:* Submission is not the same as confirmed delivery. Checking for the accepted confirmation prevents a missed payday.

7. **File the payroll register and update records** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Save the final payroll register, the exception list, the sign-off, and the confirmation number together in [where the payroll records are filed], in this period's folder.
   - **b.** Mark this period complete on the payroll calendar or tracker.
   - **c.** Write down any open items (a pending correction, a new hire missing paperwork, a held line) with an owner and a due date before the next reminder date.

   *Why this matters:* A complete, filed record for every period turns a tax filing, an audit, or a pay dispute into a quick lookup.

## Exceptions and troubleshooting

- **If** A payroll error is discovered after submission but before settlement. **Then:** Contact your payroll provider immediately. Some platforms can void or correct a submission before the funds settle; find out whether yours can and know its same-day correction deadline in advance. Record the correction and a new sign-off, and file both.
- **If** An error is discovered after the payment has already settled. **Then:** Underpayments are corrected with an off-cycle payment, not held until the next regular cycle. Overpayments are typically recovered on the next payroll cycle, but wage-deduction rules vary by jurisdiction, so confirm your local requirements with your payroll provider or an employment attorney, and get the employee's written acknowledgment before recouping.
- **If** A contractor is misclassified or a new hire is missing tax paperwork at cutoff. **Then:** Hold that individual's payment out of the batch rather than delay the whole run, tell the Second Reviewer, and resolve the paperwork before the next cycle. Never guess at a classification to make a deadline.
- **If** Payroll totals differ from the prior period by more than the variance limit with no obvious cause. **Then:** Stop before submitting. Reconcile headcount and hours against the roster line by line; an unexplained difference often points to a duplicate entry or a missed termination. Write the finding next to the register, then return to step 5.
- **If** The cutoff will be missed (no Second Reviewer is available, or the data is late). **Then:** Do not submit an unreviewed payroll. Tell the Owner, ask [your payroll provider] what the options are for a late submission, and tell affected employees the expected pay date in writing the same business day.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Cutoff reminder sent [N] business days before the deadline
- [ ] All hours are Manager-approved, not estimated; missing punches, duplicates, and overtime over the limit were returned to the Manager
- [ ] Contractor invoices matched to rates on file with no unresolved differences
- [ ] New hires, terminations, and rate changes reflected with correct effective dates
- [ ] Exceptions applied only from the documented exception list
- [ ] Payroll register reviewed and signed off by the Second Reviewer, not the person who entered the data
- [ ] Any total that differs from the prior period by more than the variance limit has a written explanation
- [ ] Submitted total equals the signed-off total, and the confirmation number is recorded; the settlement date matches the pay date
- [ ] Payroll register, exception list, sign-off, and confirmation number filed together

## Common mistakes

- **Mistake:** The same person enters the hours and approves the final submission. **Fix:** Require the Second Reviewer's sign-off in step 5 for every payroll run, even if it is a short scan of the register.
- **Mistake:** Processing payroll with estimated or unapproved hours to hit the cutoff. **Fix:** Do not enter hours without Manager approval. Hold that person's line and return it to the Manager rather than guess at hours.
- **Mistake:** Making a one-off exception (a bonus, a rate change) verbally without documenting it. **Fix:** Put every exception on the documented list for that period before it is entered, along with who approved it.
- **Mistake:** Not confirming submission transmitted before moving on. **Fix:** Check for the confirmation number or accepted status in step 6 on every run. A failed transmission looks identical to a successful one until payday.
- **Mistake:** Changing the payroll after the Second Reviewer signed off, then submitting. **Fix:** Compare the submitted total to the signed-off total in step 6. Any change after sign-off goes back to step 5.

## How to know it is working

- Every pay period is submitted before cutoff with zero unapproved hours in the batch.
- The Second Reviewer signs off on the register before every submission, with no exceptions.
- Payroll errors caught before settlement outnumber errors caught after settlement.
- Every period's register, exception list, and sign-off are filed and retrievable within minutes.
- No repeat of the same root-cause error across two consecutive pay periods.

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| On-time submission rate | 100% of runs submitted before cutoff |
| Payroll errors caught pre-submission vs. post-settlement | Pre-submission catches trending toward 100% |
| Second Reviewer sign-off completion | 100% of runs, no exceptions |
| Off-cycle correction runs per quarter | Trending down toward zero |

## Make it your procedure

1. Have the person who does this work fill in the [bracketed] parts and correct the steps. They know the details an owner skips.
2. Hand it to someone who has never done the task and watch them follow it without help. Every question they ask is a missing step. Add it.
3. When the work changes, change the procedure first, update the date under the title, and tell the people who use it what changed. Then nobody has a reason to work around it.
4. Save it with the others in one shared procedures folder, and fill in the procedure record at the bottom.
