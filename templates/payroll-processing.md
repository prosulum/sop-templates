# Payroll Processing

Updated September 28, 2026

This procedure runs one payroll cycle from the hours cutoff reminder through submission and filing of the records, for both W-2 employees and 1099 contractors. It exists so people are paid the right amount on the right day, and so no one person both enters the pay data and approves the submission. The Payroll Admin collects and enters the data, a named Second Reviewer checks the payroll register and signs off, and the Payroll Admin submits and files. Run it once every pay period, starting on the reminder date on your payroll calendar (weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly).

**What this gives you:** Payday runs the same checked way every pay period, so the people who depend on you are paid right and on time and can plan their lives around it. That makes you an employer people can trust with their livelihood.

**Primary owner:** Payroll Admin  
**Runs:** Every pay period, on your published payroll calendar  
**Time:** [e.g. 1 to 2] hours per pay period; longer on the first run or when adding new hires; replace with your own after three timed runs

- Access to [your payroll platform] with the correct user role

- A documented exception list for the pay period: bonuses, commissions, deductions, garnishments, expense reimbursements
- The payroll calendar with the reminder date, hours cutoff, submission deadline, and pay date for every period of the year
- Roles used below (rename to match your team): Payroll Admin, Second Reviewer (a named person other than the Payroll Admin), Manager (approves hours for their team), Owner (approves exceptions and decides on a missed submission deadline)
- A backup Second Reviewer, named on your worksheet

- Overtime limit, set in advance: [N2] hours per person per period, above which a Manager must confirm
- Variance limit, set in advance: [variance limit, e.g. 5 percent] between this period's payroll total and the prior period's
- Review lead time, set in advance: [N3] hours before the submission deadline by which the Second Reviewer must have signed off
- [Where the payroll records are filed, e.g. one folder on your shared drive] for the register, exception list, and sign-off
- [Where the open items list for each pay period is kept]
- [Your rate and change log], where each approved rate change, raise, and pay change is recorded with its effective date
- Your pay policy for late hours and late contractor invoices: [next pay period or off-cycle payment]

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the payroll calendar and send the hours cutoff reminder** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Open the payroll calendar and find this period's hours cutoff, submission deadline, and pay date.
   - **b.** On the reminder date, [N1] business days before the hours cutoff, send the hours cutoff reminder to Managers and hourly employees.
   - **c.** List any new hires, terminations, or pay changes that must be reflected this cycle, and note where each one is documented.

   > **Use this wording: Hours cutoff reminder to Managers and hourly employees**
   > 
   > Subject: Payroll hours cutoff [hours cutoff date] for pay date [pay date]
   > 
   > Hello [name or team],
   > 
   > Payroll for [pay period start] to [pay period end] closes for hours on [hours cutoff date] at [time]. By then, we need approved hours for every person on your team. We also need any bonus, commission, or reimbursement to include this period, and the name of who approved it. Send them to [Payroll Admin name] at [where to send].
   > 
   > Hours that are estimated or not approved cannot be entered, and payment for them moves to [next pay period or off-cycle payment, per your pay policy for late hours].
   > 
   > Thank you,\
   > [Payroll Admin name]

   *Why this matters:* A missed hours cutoff either delays pay or forces an off-cycle correction; a reminder before the cutoff is a simple way to avoid both.

2. **Collect and verify approved hours** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Pull hours from [your time tracking system] or collect the Manager-approved timesheets.
   - **b.** Check each person's hours for missing punches, duplicate entries, and overtime above the overtime limit.
   - **c.** For each problem found, remove that person's line from this batch, send it back to the Manager with the message below, and record it as held in [the open items list for this pay period]. Continue with the remaining lines.
   - **d.** Do not accept estimated or unapproved hours. Return them to the Manager for sign-off first, in the same way.
   - **e.** When a Manager returns a corrected, approved line before the hours cutoff, add it back to the batch and continue with step 3.
   - **f.** If the corrected line has not come back by the hours cutoff, leave it out of this batch and tell the Second Reviewer it is held. Pay it in the next period or in an off-cycle payment, as set by [your pay policy for late hours and late contractor invoices].

   > **Use this wording: Hours returned to a Manager**
   > 
   > Hello [Manager name],
   > 
   > I have held [employee name]'s hours for [pay period] out of this payroll because [reason: a missing punch on a date, a duplicate entry on a date, or overtime above the limit, with the hours]. Please send corrected, approved hours by [hours cutoff date and time]. If they arrive after that, they will be paid in [next pay period or off-cycle payment].
   > 
   > Thank you,\
   > [Payroll Admin name]

   *Why this matters:* Unapproved or estimated hours can be wrong, and a wrong entry is harder to fix after money has moved.

3. **Verify contractor invoices, new hires, rate changes, and terminations** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Match every contractor invoice against the rate and scope on file. If one does not match, hold that invoice out of the batch and send it back to the contractor with the difference stated.
   - **b.** When the contractor sends a corrected invoice before the hours cutoff, match it again and add it to the batch. If it has not come back by then, leave it out and pay it under [your pay policy for late hours and late contractor invoices].
   - **c.** Confirm each new hire is fully set up in [your payroll platform] (classification, tax withholding forms, direct deposit) before their first pay run, following the Hiring Process procedure. If setup is not complete, hold that person out of this batch and tell the Second Reviewer.
   - **d.** Add the person to the batch if setup is complete before the hours cutoff. Otherwise list them in [the open items list for this pay period] in step 7.
   - **e.** Apply each approved rate change or raise effective this period, and confirm the effective date matches [your rate and change log].
   - **f.** Apply each termination effective this period, including final pay items, following the Employee Offboarding procedure.

   *Why this matters:* A classification or rate error carries into every pay period until someone corrects it, so check them at the source.

4. **Enter hours and apply documented exceptions** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Enter the approved hours, salaries, and contractor payments into [your payroll platform].
   - **b.** Apply exceptions (bonuses, commissions, deductions, garnishments, reimbursements) only from the documented exception list for this period, not from memory or a side conversation. If someone asks for an exception that is not on the list, decline it for this period and ask them to get it approved and added.
   - **c.** Save a copy of the exception list to [where the payroll records are filed] with this period's payroll records.

   *Why this matters:* A documented exception list creates an audit trail and keeps "just this once" changes on the record.

5. **Run a second-person review and get sign-off before submission** (Owner: Second Reviewer)

   - **a.** Open the payroll register. Check headcount, hours per person, pay rates, deductions, and total cost.
   - **b.** Compare this period's total to the prior period's. If the difference is more than the variance limit, find the cause in the payroll data before you continue, and write the explanation next to the register. If you cannot find the cause, follow the variance entry in the troubleshooting section below.
   - **c.** If everything is explained, sign off in [where the Second Reviewer signs off, e.g. an approval in the payroll platform or a signed email], with your name and the date.
   - **d.** If you find an error, return the register to the Payroll Admin with the error written down. The Payroll Admin corrects it in step 4, and you repeat this step from the beginning.
   - **e.** If you have not signed off by [N3] hours before the submission deadline, the Payroll Admin sends the register to the backup Second Reviewer. If neither can review before the submission deadline, do not submit without a review. Follow the missed submission deadline entry in the troubleshooting section below.

   *Why this matters:* A second reviewer checks what the preparer is too close to see. It is the check that does not depend on the preparer being right.

6. **Submit payroll and confirm transmission** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Check that the payroll total on screen equals the total the Second Reviewer signed off. If it differs, do not submit; return to step 5.
   - **b.** Submit payroll in [your payroll platform] before the submission deadline and record the confirmation number or submission timestamp.
   - **c.** Confirm that direct deposit transmissions were accepted and that contractor ACH or check payments were started. If any were rejected, fix the bank detail with the employee or contractor by phone or in person, then resubmit that payment and record the new confirmation.
   - **d.** Check that the expected settlement date matches the pay date on the calendar. If it does not, contact [your payroll provider] the same business day.

   *Why this matters:* Submission is not the same as confirmed delivery. Checking for the accepted confirmation lets you catch a failed payment before payday.

7. **File the payroll register and update records** (Owner: Payroll Admin)

   - **a.** Save the final payroll register, the exception list, the sign-off, and the confirmation number together in [where the payroll records are filed], in this period's folder.
   - **b.** Mark this period complete on the payroll calendar.
   - **c.** Write down any open items (a pending correction, a new hire missing paperwork, a held line) in [the open items list for this pay period], with an owner and a due date before the next reminder date.

   *Why this matters:* A complete, filed record for every period turns a tax filing, an audit, or a pay dispute into a quick lookup.

## Exceptions and troubleshooting

- **If** A payroll error is discovered after submission but before settlement. **Then:** Contact your payroll provider immediately. Some platforms can void or correct a submission before the funds settle; find out whether yours can and know its same-day correction deadline in advance. Record the correction and a new sign-off, and file both.
- **If** An error is discovered after the payment has already settled. **Then:** Correct an underpayment with an off-cycle payment rather than holding it until the next regular cycle, unless your payroll provider or local rules say otherwise. Recover an overpayment on the next payroll cycle only if the rules allow it. Wage-deduction rules vary by jurisdiction, so confirm your local requirements with your payroll provider or an employment attorney, and get the employee's written acknowledgment before recouping.
- **If** A contractor is misclassified or a new hire is missing tax paperwork at the hours cutoff. **Then:** Hold that individual's payment out of the batch rather than delay the whole run, tell the Second Reviewer, and resolve the paperwork before the next cycle. Never guess at a classification to make a deadline.
- **If** Payroll totals differ from the prior period by more than the variance limit with no obvious cause. **Then:** Stop before submitting. Reconcile headcount and hours against the roster line by line; an unexplained difference can point to a duplicate entry or a missed termination. Write the finding next to the register, then return to step 5.
- **If** The submission deadline will be missed (no Second Reviewer is available, or the data is late). **Then:** Do not submit an unreviewed payroll. Tell the Owner, ask [your payroll provider] what the options are for a late submission, and tell affected employees the expected pay date in writing the same business day.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Hours cutoff reminder sent [N1] business days before the hours cutoff
- [ ] All hours are Manager-approved, not estimated; missing punches, duplicates, and overtime over the limit were returned to the Manager
- [ ] Contractor invoices matched to rates on file with no unresolved differences
- [ ] New hires, terminations, and rate changes reflected with correct effective dates
- [ ] Exceptions applied only from the documented exception list
- [ ] Payroll register reviewed and signed off by the Second Reviewer, not the person who entered the data
- [ ] Any total that differs from the prior period by more than the variance limit has a written explanation
- [ ] Submitted total equals the signed-off total, and the confirmation number is recorded; the settlement date matches the pay date
- [ ] Payroll register, exception list, sign-off, and confirmation number filed together

## Common mistakes

- **Mistake:** The same person enters the hours and approves the final submission. **Fix:** Require the Second Reviewer's sign-off in step 5 for every payroll run, even if it is a short scan of the register.
- **Mistake:** Processing payroll with estimated or unapproved hours to hit the hours cutoff. **Fix:** Do not enter hours without Manager approval. Hold that person's line and return it to the Manager rather than guess at hours.
- **Mistake:** Making a one-off exception (a bonus, a rate change) verbally without documenting it. **Fix:** Put every exception on the documented list for that period before it is entered, along with who approved it.
- **Mistake:** Not confirming submission transmitted before moving on. **Fix:** Check for the confirmation number or accepted status in step 6 on every run. A failed transmission can look like a successful one until payday.
- **Mistake:** Changing the payroll after the Second Reviewer signed off, then submitting. **Fix:** Compare the submitted total to the signed-off total in step 6. Any change after sign-off goes back to step 5.

## How to know it is working

- Every pay period is submitted by the submission deadline with zero unapproved hours in the batch.
- The Second Reviewer signs off on the register before every submission, with no exceptions.
- Payroll errors caught before settlement outnumber errors caught after settlement.
- Every period's register, exception list, and sign-off are filed and retrievable within minutes.
- No repeat of the same root-cause error across two consecutive pay periods.

## Make it your procedure

1. Have the person who does this work fill in the [bracketed] parts and correct the steps. They know the details an owner skips.
2. Hand it to someone who has never done the task and watch them follow it without help. Every question they ask is a missing step. Add it.
3. When the work changes, change the procedure first, update the date under the title, and tell the people who use it what changed. Then nobody has a reason to work around it.
4. Save it with the others in one shared procedures folder, and fill in the procedure record at the bottom.
